CubanBucketList.com – Business Proposition, Deep Dive & Fleshed-Out Vision
Your idea is strong and timely. Experiential travel is booming (people are tired of generic beach resorts and want real, story-worthy moments), and Cuba is one of the most photogenic, culturally rich, and still-undiscovered destinations on the planet. Most visitors only see Havana’s classic cars and Varadero beaches. You’re positioning yourself as the insider gateway to the Cuba that locals actually love—mountains, family kitchens, secret dive spots, hidden waterfalls, etc.
Why this works

Low-risk launch: Newsletter + simple site = almost zero overhead. You build an email list and real demand signals before you commit to operations, bookings, insurance, or payments.
Natural validation loop: Every newsletter issue and experience spotlight becomes market research. High open/click rates + “I want this” form submissions tell you exactly which experiences to productize first.
High-margin future: Bespoke, small-group or 1:1 experiences (photography trek, private chef night, private scuba) can command premium pricing ($800–$2,500+ per person) because they feel exclusive and deeply personal.
Moat: Your network of “really cool locals” (photographers, chefs, instructors) is the asset. If you treat them well and give them a fair cut, they become your best marketers and quality control.
Brand name is perfect: “Cuban Bucket List” instantly communicates aspiration, adventure, and the “must-do before I die” vibe.

Target audience (primary)

28–55 years old
Culturally curious, creative, or active travelers (photographers, foodies, divers, hikers, writers)
Mid-to-high income, willing to pay for quality and uniqueness
Mostly from US, Canada, EU (Americans especially love the “support for the Cuban people” compliant angle)
They hate group tours and cruise-ship vibes

Core brand promise
“Real Cuba, real people, experiences you’ll actually tell your friends about.”
Tone: Warm, respectful, adventurous, slightly poetic. Never salesy. Celebrate Cuban creativity, resilience, and generosity.
Phased rollout (what you sketched, made concrete)

Now – Phase 1 (Content & Community)
Free weekly or bi-weekly newsletter + beautiful website hub.
Content types:
Hidden gem spotlights (one per issue)
Local hero profiles (the photographer, the chef, the scuba guide)
Beautiful photo essays + stories
“A Day in the Life” dispatches
Simple polls: “Which experience are you most excited about?”

Phase 2 (Interest Gauging – 3–6 months)
Add waitlists, “Express Interest” forms, and light lead magnets on the site.
Top-voted experiences get turned into proper packages first.
Phase 3 (Monetization)
Paid small-group or private experiences.
Possible future products: multi-day itineraries, custom trips, premium newsletter, affiliate lodging partnerships.

Risks & smart mitigations (being honest)

Cuba’s logistics/politics/infrastructure: Be transparent. Partner only with reliable locals who have track records.
US travel rules: Always frame experiences as “support for the Cuban people” (legal category). Your lawyer/future operator should confirm compliance.
Quality control: Start small, vet everyone personally, collect feedback religiously.
Competition: There are some Cuba specialists, but almost none focus purely on hyper-local, creative, one-of-a-kind experiences like yours.

Website goals (what the v0 mockup needs to achieve)

Instantly convey beauty + authenticity with big, emotional Cuba photography.
Make newsletter signup the #1 action (big, repeated, low-friction).
Showcase experiences in a way that makes people feel the excitement and want to raise their hand.
Feel premium but warm—not corporate travel-agency sterile.
